# /devil

## Trigger

Use after `/sales` or before any material public commercial, merchandising, checkout, claim, campaign, analytics, or storefront decision.

## Two attacks

### Premise attack

Challenge whether the customer problem, demand, differentiation, proof, channel, timing, and expected outcome are real. Identify missing evidence, excluded customers, accessibility risk, privacy risk, support burden, fulfillment risk, and reasons the work should not proceed.

### Plan attack

Challenge the selected implementation for misleading claims, inaccessible presentation, stale price or stock state, deceptive urgency, hidden cost, checkout confusion, client-authoritative payment state, catalog contamination, provider dependence, data leakage, deployment drift, and unauthorized execution.

Define measurable success, failure, and kill criteria. Prefer the smallest reversible test that preserves customer agency and founder authority.

## Authority boundary

This review **does not authorize** pricing, discounts, products, inventory, claims, analytics, communication, checkout, deployment, customer-data use, refunds, publication, or rollback. A passing adversarial review is evidence for a founder decision, not the decision itself.

## Output

Return premise risks, plan risks, strongest counterargument, missing proof, kill criteria, safe reversible alternative, rollback, and the exact next founder gate.
